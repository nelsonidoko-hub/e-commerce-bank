# Template Studio: how to make new templates

The Template Studio lets you and your team make new templates **without code**, by choosing from the parts the storefront already has. Templates go into the gallery next to the 8 built-in ones, each with its own demo shop.

---

## 1. Who can use it

- **You (the founder):** everything, including **Publish** and **Hide**.
- **Designers (your team):** make templates, edit them, and **Send for review**. They can't publish, and they can't see money, shops or customers.

**To add a designer:**
1. They sign up on the platform like a merchant (any shop name is fine).
2. Go to **Founder → Team**, type their email and press **Add designer**.
3. They log out and back in. **Studio** then appears in their menu.

---

## 2. Making a template, step by step

1. Open **Studio → New template**.
2. **Start from:** pick the built-in template closest to what you want. All its settings are copied, so you only change what's different.
3. **Name and description:** what merchants read in the gallery.
   - *Template name:* e.g. "Luxe"
   - *Short name:* filled in for you (`luxe`). It **can't change later**, because shops refer to it.
   - *Best for:* e.g. "Jewellery and luxury shops"
   - *Description:* one sentence, e.g. "Black and gold, elegant serif headings, centred products."
4. **Sample products:** what a new shop starts with. Each product comes with a picture that shows what it is: 👗 for dresses, 🍛 for jollof, 📱 for phones. The preview and the demo shop use these too, so non-technical merchants can see the template "with things in it".
5. **Colours:**
   - *Accent:* buttons and links. Pick something dark enough for white text on it.
   - *Dark text and buttons:* optional.
   - *Page background:* optional. Leave it as "None" for white.
6. **Fonts and corners:** headings, body text, heading thickness, CAPITALS on or off, and square, slightly rounded or very rounded corners.
7. **Product cards:** the biggest change to the look. Each option is explained in plain words:
   - Standard, Clean, Boxed, Menu row, Compact, Centred, Spec sheet, Tile
8. **Product layout:**
   - Grid or List (List goes with "Menu row")
   - 2–5 products per row on a computer (phones always show 2)
   - The big search box, which suits shops with many products
9. **Banner and footer:** full-screen, medium or slim-strip banner, and a dark or light footer.
10. Watch the **Live preview** on the right (at the top on a phone). It updates as you change things.
11. Press **Create draft**, then **Save** as you go.
12. When it's ready, press **Send for review**.

**You (the founder) then:**
1. Open it from **Studio**. Its status is "Waiting for review".
2. Check the preview, and change anything you like.
3. Press **Publish to gallery**. This puts it on the Templates page and in the sign-up picker, and **automatically builds a demo shop** for it (`?store=demo-luxe`). It also shows in every merchant's **Builder → Template** list, so existing shops can switch to it.

---

## 2a. "My own products": for any kind of shop

The 8 ready-made sample sets (clothing, food, gadgets…) don't fit every shop. For farm produce, a pharmacy, a bakery, books and so on, choose **Sample products → My own products (type them in)**:
- **Banner heading and line:** e.g. "Straight from the farm", "Harvested this week".
- **Categories:** these become the **shop's menu** (up to 8), e.g. Vegetables, Fruits, Grains & Tubers, Livestock.
- **Products:** up to 12, each with a name, price, category, short description and a **photo**.

The preview, the demo shop and every new shop made with the template then start with exactly these. Switching to "My own products" starts you off with the previous set's products (and any photos you already uploaded), so you can rename them rather than start empty.

## 2b. Adding real photos

Every template, new or built-in, has a **Photos (optional)** section in the editor:
- **Top banner:** the big picture at the top of the shop.
- **Middle banner** and **Featured picture**.
- **One slot per sample product** (e.g. "Party Jollof Rice", "Chilled Zobo"). The list changes with the sample products you chose.

Press **Upload** on a slot, pick a photo from your phone or computer (up to 5 MB), then **Save**. The live preview shows it straight away. Anything without a photo keeps its drawn picture, so you can add photos bit by bit.

When the template is published, **new shops and the demo shop start with your photos**. Merchants then replace them with pictures of their own products. Shops that already exist keep their own pictures; photos only affect new shops and the demo.

> ⚠️ **Only use photos you're allowed to use:** your own, ones you paid for, or free-licence stock from sites like **Unsplash** or **Pexels**. Never copy pictures from other shops, Instagram or Google Images. You could get copyright complaints against the platform.

**Good photo tips:** use bright, clear photos on a plain background. Banners should be wide (landscape) and product photos square or slightly tall. Photos of Nigerian food, fashion and products make merchants feel at home.

## 2c. Editing the 8 built-in templates

In **Studio**, the **Built-in templates** row shows all 8 with an **Edit** button.
1. Press **Edit** on one (e.g. Fresh). An "edited version" opens, starting exactly like the original.
2. Change anything: colours, fonts, card style, layout, banner, and add photos.
3. Send it for review, then (founder) **Publish to gallery**.
4. Once published, **every shop using that template gets the new look**, the gallery card shows it, its demo shop is rebuilt with it, and new shops start with its photos.
5. Changed your mind? **Reset to original** (founder) deletes the edit, and everything goes back to the original look. Shops keep working throughout.

The short name of a built-in can't change, so shops always stay on it.

## 2d. Page structure: templates that are really different

Colours and fonts only change the *look*. **Page structure** changes the *skeleton*, meaning where the menu, banner and products go. In the editor, the **Page structure** section has three choices:

| Part | Options |
|---|---|
| **Menu bar** | Standard (categories, logo, cart) · **Centred logo** (big logo in the middle, menu underneath, fashion) · **Search bar** (logo, wide search bar and cart, categories underneath, electronics) |
| **Banner** | Full screen · Medium · Slim strip · **Split screen** (photo one side, words the other, fashion) · **No banner** (products straight away) |
| **Homepage** | Standard · **Lookbook** (big "shop by category" photos first, fashion) · **Sidebar catalog** (category list beside the products, electronics) · **Single product** (from the Spotlight pack: one big product with Buy now) |

Two new built-in templates show what this can do:
- **Gadget** (electronics): search-bar menu, no banner, sidebar catalog, spec-sheet product cards.
- **Atelier** (fashion): centred logo, split-screen banner, lookbook homepage, clean product cards and a serif font.

Use **Start from → Gadget or Atelier** to make your own versions. The original 8 templates keep the standard structure, so existing shops look exactly as before.

Phones get their own version of each part: the sidebar becomes a row of category chips, and the split banner stacks the photo above the words.

### The rest of the shop

Below the banner, every template used to be built the same way. Now the editor has four more sections, so the *whole* shop can differ:

| Section | Options |
|---|---|
| **Homepage sections → Product rows** | Grid · **Sliding row** · **Spotlight** (first product big, four small beside it) · **Shelf** (small photo beside name and price, supermarket style) |
| **Homepage sections → Shop by category** | None · **Big photos** · **Circles** (swipe through round photos) · **Buttons** · **Wide banners** with "Shop now" |
| **Homepage sections → Customer reviews** | Cards · **One big quote** (with dots) · **Magazine** (italic, thin line beside) |
| **Product page** | Classic (photo left, details right) · **Wide photos** (photos across the top) · **Tall gallery** (all photos down the page, buy box stays in view) · **Simple** (one column, food and small shops) |
| **Shop & category pages** | Standard filter row · **Sidebar filters** (on phones: category chips plus a fold-away filter) · **Filter buttons** · **Big photos** (two per row) |
| **Spacing & feel** | Compact · Normal · Airy, plus **coloured bands** behind every other section and **thin lines** between sections |

A merchant who sets a product row to "Sliding row" in their own builder always gets a sliding row. Their other rows follow the template's style.

**The built-in templates now use these too**, so each one feels different all the way through. For example, Catalog has shelf rows, category circles, sidebar filters and compact spacing; Atelier has spotlight rows, a tall-gallery product page and big-photo shop pages; Fresh has category buttons, coloured bands and a simple product page. **Classic is unchanged.** Shops on the other templates will see the new structure after you deploy.

## 2e. Template packs: totally custom designs (for a developer)

When a design needs something the parts can't do (a one-off look from a Figma file, a new kind of homepage, a new menu bar), a developer builds a **template pack**:

1. Copy the example pack `frontend/src/storefront/packs/spotlight/` to a new folder, e.g. `packs/runway/`.
2. In its `index.js`, give it an id and list its parts:
   ```js
   export default {
     id: 'runway',
     label: 'Runway',
     parts: {
       home: { runway: { label: 'Runway homepage', help: 'Full-screen video, then products', Component: RunwayHome } },
       nav:  { 'runway-nav': { label: 'Runway menu', help: 'Transparent menu with a side drawer', Component: RunwayNav } },
     },
   }
   ```
3. Write the components. A **homepage** gets `{ settings, sections, renderSection, products }`: draw anything, and call `renderSection(section)` to reuse the merchant's own sections (product rows, banners, testimonials…). A **menu bar** is a normal React component.
4. Add the pack to the list in `packs/index.js` and deploy the frontend.
5. The new parts appear in the Studio under **Page structure**. You then make the template there (colours, fonts, photos) and publish it like any other.

No backend change is needed. Part ids are short names (letters, numbers, dashes). If a pack is ever removed, shops using its parts fall back to the standard menu and homepage, so they never break.

## 2f. Client work: private templates and shops made for clients

**Private templates.** In the editor, under **Who can use it**, choose **Private (for clients)** and enter the client's email(s). A private template:
- never shows in the gallery and gets no public demo shop;
- can only be used by those accounts (another merchant can't switch to it, even if they know its name);
- shows in that client's Builder as **"Your own template"**.

**Create a shop for a client.** Go to **Founder → Clients**, enter the client's name, email and shop name, pick a template (built-in, public or private), and press **Create shop for client**.
- **New client:** a platform account is made in their name, and they get an email: *"Your shop is ready"*, with a link to choose their password and accept the terms (valid 7 days; after that they can use "Forgot password"). You never know their password.
- **Existing client:** the shop is added to their account (it appears under My shops). No email is sent.
- Then use **Open its page** to give free months if that's part of your deal.

Every shop made this way is logged in the Founder actions.

## 2g. The editor works like the builder: live shop preview, homepage and words

The Studio editor now has four tabs on the left and the **real shop** on the right (laptop or phone view):

| Tab | What you set |
|---|---|
| **Details** | Name, who can use it, sample products, photos |
| **Look** | Colours, fonts, corners |
| **Layout** | Menu bar, banner, homepage layout, product cards, product page, shop pages, spacing, footer |
| **Homepage & words** | The banner's words (heading, small text, button text and where it goes), the **button words** ("Add to order", "Order now"), and the **homepage sections**, which you add, reorder, hide and edit exactly like the merchant's builder |

The preview is a demo shop with the same sample products, so every change shows instantly. Type `{shop}` in any text and each shop sees its own name there ("Hot meals from {shop}" becomes "Hot meals from Mama Put").

What happens with it:
- **New shops** start with the template's homepage, banner words and button words, so a food shop says "Order now" from day one.
- **Merchants can change everything** in their own builder. The button words are under Builder → Design → Button words.
- **The built-in templates each have their own homepage now.** For example, Fresh has Today's menu, How to order, Small chops, Drinks, reviews and Order on WhatsApp; Tech has Phones, a trade-in banner, Audio, Accessories and Why buy from us.
- Pictures left empty in a section get a sample picture of one of the template's own products.
- A section for a category the shop doesn't have shows the newest products instead of an empty row.
- A template with **My own products** gets its own preview shop when you press Save, so the preview shows your products.

**After deploying, run `npm run seed-demos` once** so every demo shop is rebuilt with its template's new homepage. Shops that already exist keep the homepage they have.

## 2h. Merchants can keep your products and pictures

- **Removing the samples is no longer forced.** The setup step is now "Keep or remove the sample products" and has two buttons:
  - **Keep them:** the template's products, categories, banner and section pictures become the shop's own. The "SAMPLE" mark disappears from drawn pictures, and the products count as real ones for publishing and the Market Square. The merchant should check prices and stock afterwards.
  - **Remove:** works as before.
- **Editing and saving a sample product** also makes it the merchant's own.
- **Your template can bring up to 60 products and 20 categories** under "My own products" (it was 12 and 8). Add a photo for each one in the Photos section.
- **Builder → Design** now has **"Use this template's homepage"**. A merchant who switches template can also take its homepage. It goes into their draft first, and nothing changes for customers until they publish.

## 4. Changing, hiding and deleting

- **Editing a published template** (founder only) changes **every shop using it** at once. That's useful for fixes, but be careful.
- **Hide from gallery:** new merchants can't pick it any more, but shops already using it keep it.
- **Delete:** only for templates no shop is using. Use Hide for the rest.
- The list shows how many shops use each template.

## 5. For a developer: adding brand-new parts

Some looks can't be made from the current parts, such as a split-screen banner, a video banner, a logo-in-the-middle menu or a new card style. A developer adds the part **once**, and it then appears in the Studio for every future template.

**A new product card style** (e.g. `magazine`):
1. `frontend/src/utils/themes.js`: add `'magazine'` to `CARD_STYLES`.
2. `frontend/src/components/ProductCard.jsx`: add how a `magazine` card looks.
3. `frontend/src/components/ThemePreview.jsx`: add its small version (`cardClass` / `imageClass`).
4. `frontend/src/platform/pages/studio/shared.jsx`: give it a plain-English name in `PART_LABELS.card`.
5. `backend/utils/templates.js`: add `'magazine'` to `PARTS.card`.
6. Run the tests. A test checks that the frontend and backend lists match.

**A new banner style:** the same steps, with `hero` instead of `card`, plus the banner component (`components/Hero.jsx` / `components/sections/Sections.jsx`).

**A new font:** add it to the Google Fonts link in `index.html`, then to `FONT_STACKS`, `FONT_LABELS` and `HEADING_FONTS`/`BODY_FONTS` in `themes.js`, `PART_LABELS` in the Studio, and `PARTS` plus `HEADING_FONTS`/`BODY_FONTS` in the backend (`utils/templates.js`, `controllers/settingsController.js`).

**A new built-in template in code** (rarely needed now): add it to `TEMPLATES` in `themes.js`, `BUILTIN_TEMPLATES` in `backend/utils/templates.js`, a sample set in `backend/utils/sampleData.js`, `PREVIEW_SETS` in `frontend/src/utils/pictureIcons.js`, and a demo name in `scripts/seedDemos.js`. Then redeploy and run `npm run seed-demos`.
